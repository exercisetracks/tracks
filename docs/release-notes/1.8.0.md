### Watch sync
- Syncs no longer wedge until the app is force-stopped: stalled transfers time out, and a failed sync rebuilds the watch link and tries again by itself.
- Syncing while the watch is recording an activity skips that activity quietly and fetches it once you stop.
- While you record on the watch, your track shows live on the phone's map.
- A sync trace can be shared from Settings → Watch when something goes wrong.

### Training and workouts
- Start any activity from the dashboard — run, ride, hike, strength, flexibility and more — with your most frequent one picked for you, an intensity slider and optional muscles to focus on.
- Planned strength and flexibility sessions can be shortened or lengthened before you start.
- Strength sessions use supersets where nobody has to cross the gym: lifts that share dumbbells or a bar, or need no equipment, go in turn with a rest after each.
- Every lift shows the weight to use. A lift you have never logged starts from a guess based on your body weight, marked "starting guess"; the weights you log replace it.
- Log the weight and reps of each set on the phone as you go; weights entered on the watch count too.
- Rest between sets runs one to three minutes depending on the movement; moderate sessions are 8 reps for compound lifts and 12 for single-joint ones.
- "RPE" is gone: sets show a weight, or the effort in words ("effort 7/10, 3 reps in the tank").
- Each stretch is held for as long as it needs — longer for deep hip and hamstring stretches, shorter for balance and strength poses — about a minute on average at moderate.
- Every pose and lift now has real step-by-step instructions, shown large under its name during the session.
- One guided screen for every sport, with the current stage at a glance; workouts keep going with the screen off.
- Voice cues work again in every session type, including flexibility. Skipping stops the voice at once, and pausing and resuming are spoken.
- The training plan adapts to training it did not plan: after a big unplanned day it shortens, eases, moves or drops the next sessions, and rebuilds after every new activity.
- The dashboard shows each sport's own fitness, fatigue and form when you pick it in the pie chart.

### Health
- New: intensity minutes (weekly) and floors climbed, on the phone and the web. Past days fill in by themselves.
- Set your own step, intensity-minute and floor goals in Settings, or leave them on Auto, which follows what you usually do within sensible limits.
- A heart-rate dial: live on the phone while the watch is worn (blank when it is not), with the whole day's heart rate behind it.
- Sleep opens on last night and draws stages where the watch recorded them.
- Every history chart is drawn in its own coloured bands; the ring past a goal is gold.

### Maps
- Parks, gardens and fields are green and named early; park names win over the shelters inside them.
- Each trail is drawn and named once, trails are named close in, and ski runs and nordic trails appear as areas are rebuilt.
- Peaks, springs, campgrounds and other places load with the map and work offline on the phone.
- Your activities draw under the labels, roads and trails fade in, and your layer choices are remembered.
- The web map makes far fewer requests and no longer polls while idle; 3D is lighter.
- On the phone in 3D, taps land where you touch, activities and route lines show, and the map is sharp.

### Phone
- Status pops up briefly and then goes; the drawer keeps running jobs, the latest success for a minute, and errors until they clear.
- Activities: search filters as you type, nine sorts in one line, and sorting applies at once.
- Activity stats fit their boxes, with smaller units.
- Map windows share one floating look; the music settings take a few lines.
- Signing in says why it failed.
